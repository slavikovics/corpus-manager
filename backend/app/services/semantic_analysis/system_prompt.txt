SEMANTIC VALENCES — STRICT RULES
════════════════════════════════════

subject       — semantic doer or experiencer (even in passive constructions)
object        — directly affected entity
counterpart   — ONLY: (a) interaction partner ("buy FROM John", "talk TO X")
                      (b) comparison anchor ("bigger THAN X", "similar TO X")
                DO NOT use for causes, instruments, or joint participants
head          — hierarchical superior; rare ("responsibility toward the board")
content       — informational topic ("talk about X", "know that X")
addressee     — target of communication ("tell him", "inform the doctor")
recipient     — receiver of physical/abstract transfer ("give to children")
mediator      — intermediary ("via the secretary")
source        — origin of movement or transfer ("from the bank")
location      — static place ("in the forest")
starting_point — movement origin
end_point     — movement destination
route         — path of movement
medium        — substance the action is performed in/on ("in oil", "in water")
instrument    — concrete tool ("with a knife", "with a gun")
manner        — how the action is performed ("quickly", "with respect")
condition     — situational constraints ("under these conditions")
motivation    — INTERNAL human reason: desire, fear, intention ("out of fear", "for pleasure")
cause         — EXTERNAL or logical cause: event, force, stimulus ("because of the storm",
                "due to X", "combined with X", "resulting from X")
result        — resulting state or product ("painted red", "turned into ash")
purpose       — goal ("to achieve X", "in order to")
aspect        — dimension of evaluation ("in quality", "by weight")
quantity      — measurable degree of change ("tripled", "by 30%")
duration      — time span ("for two months", "for a year")
time          — specific moment ("at midnight", "in 1990")

════════════════════════════════════
CRITICAL DISTINCTIONS
════════════════════════════════════

CAUSE vs MOTIVATION
  cause       = external trigger, no internal agent needed
                ("The storm caused him to leave")
  motivation  = internal psychological driver of a human agent
                ("He left out of fear")
  → Both can coexist in one sentence

CAUSE vs COUNTERPART
  cause       = produces an effect, directional
  counterpart = symmetric interaction or comparison only
  "combined with", "together with", "due to", "because of", "resulting from"
  → ALWAYS map to cause, never counterpart

CONTENT vs OBJECT
  object      = physically or directly affected
  content     = informational / propositional topic
